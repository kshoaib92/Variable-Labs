Potential Energy

Potential energy is stored energy. An object has it because of where it is or how it is squashed or stretched. It isn't doing anything yet, but it is ready to be released. Lift a ball and you store energy in it. Let go, and that stored energy turns into motion.

Gravitational potential energy
PE=m×g×h
mmass of the object, in kilograms (kg)
gstrength of gravity, 9.81 m/s² on Earth
hheight above the ground, in metres (m)
The answer is in joules (J). One joule is about the energy needed to lift a small apple (100 g) by one metre.
Idea 1

Higher means more

Lift something twice as high and it stores twice as much energy. Height is the "position" part of potential energy.

Idea 2

Heavier and stronger gravity mean more

A bowling ball on a shelf stores more than a tennis ball on the same shelf. The same ball stores less on the Moon, where gravity is weaker.

Idea 3

Energy changes form, never disappears

As an object falls, stored energy becomes motion (kinetic) energy. On landing it becomes heat, sound and a dent. The total stays the same.

Kinds of potential energy: Gravitational from height Elastic from stretching or squashing, ½·k·x² Chemical in food, fuel and batteries

    Kinetic Energy

    Kinetic energy is the energy of motion. Anything that moves has it: a rolling ball, a running dog, a speeding car, even the wind. The heavier something is and, above all, the faster it goes, the more kinetic energy it carries, and the harder it is to stop.

    Connects to Potential Energy: a falling ball turns stored energy into kinetic energy.
    Kinetic energy
    KE=½×m×v²
    mmass of the object, in kilograms (kg)
    vspeed, in metres per second (m/s)
    v²speed × speed. This is why speed matters so much
    The answer is in joules (J). A 2 kg ball rolling at 1 m/s has 1 J. A small car at 50 km/h carries about 100,000 J.
    Idea 1

    Speed counts twice

    Speed is squared. Go 2 times faster and you carry 4 times the energy. Go 3 times faster and it's 9 times.

    Idea 2

    Mass counts once

    A van twice as heavy as a car, at the same speed, carries twice the energy. Only twice, not four times.

    Idea 3

    Stopping means moving the energy

    To stop, all that energy has to go somewhere: heat in the brakes, or squashed metal in a crash. More energy means a longer stop or a bigger dent.

    Kinds of kinetic energy: Moving along a car, a thrown ball Spinning wheels, fans, a spinning top Thermal jiggling particles, felt as heat

      Where you meet kinetic energy

      Moving air

      Wind turbines

      Wind is moving air with kinetic energy. The blades catch it and a generator turns it into electricity.

      Road safety

      Speed limits near schools

      Dropping from 50 to 30 km/h cuts a car's kinetic energy by almost two thirds, so it stops much sooner.

      Spinning

      Electric car braking

      Electric cars use the motor as a generator when slowing down, turning kinetic energy back into battery charge.

      Impact

      Meteor craters

      A rock about 50 m across, moving at over 10 km per second, blasted out Meteor Crater in Arizona, more than a kilometre wide.

      Physics · Motion

      Pendulums

      A pendulum is a weight hanging from a string or rod that swings back and forth. One full swing, there and back, always takes the same time. That time is called the period. The steady beat made pendulums the heart of clocks for almost 300 years.

      Connects to Potential and Kinetic Energy: every swing trades one for the other.
      Period of a pendulum
      T=2π×√(L ÷ g)
      Tperiod: time for one full swing there and back, in seconds (s)
      Llength from the pivot to the middle of the bob, in metres (m)
      gstrength of gravity, 9.81 m/s² on Earth
      Notice what's missing: the mass of the bob and how far you pull it back. For normal swings, neither changes the period.
      Idea 1

      Length sets the beat

      Longer pendulums swing more slowly. Make one 4 times longer and each swing takes twice as long, because of the square root.

      Idea 2

      Mass doesn't matter

      A heavy bob and a light bob on the same length of string swing in step. Gravity pulls harder on the heavy one, but it is also harder to get moving.

      Idea 3

      Energy swaps sides

      At each end the bob stops for an instant: all stored (potential) energy. At the bottom it moves fastest: all motion (kinetic) energy.

      Where the energy is: Ends of the swing all potential energy Bottom of the swing all kinetic energy With air a little turns to heat each swing

        Where you meet pendulums

        Timekeeping

        Big Ben's clock

        Its 3.9 m pendulum ticks every 2 seconds. Engineers fine-tune it by adding or removing old pennies from the pendulum.

        Play

        Playground swings

        A swing is a pendulum. Kicking your legs at the right moment in each swing adds energy, so you go higher.

        Engineering

        Taipei 101

        This skyscraper hides a 660-tonne steel ball hung like a pendulum. It swings against strong winds to keep the tower steady.

        Discovery

        Foucault's pendulum

        A very long pendulum slowly changes the direction it swings over a day. That showed people the Earth is spinning.
